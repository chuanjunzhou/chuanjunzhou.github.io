渾儀 hunyi · the Chinese armillary sphere? What is a hunyi? Show a short historical note. Ring legend: 地平 horizon · 子午 meridian · 赤道 equator · 黃道 ecliptic · 四游雙環 double ring · 望筒 sighting tube

A 渾儀 hunyi is a set of nested graduated rings standing for circles of the sky, mounted on a polar axis tilted to the latitude of the observing site; Kaifeng, the Northern Song capital, lies near 35° north. The observer sighted a star through a straight tube in the innermost ring and read its place off the rings, in Chinese practice as a north polar distance (去極度) and a position along the equator, with the circle divided into 365¼ du (度), one for each day of the sun's yearly course, rather than 360 degrees. On this sphere the rings follow Su Song's three nests: the fixed 六合儀 liuhe yi with horizon, meridian and a fixed equator ring graduated for the hours, the 三辰儀 sanchen yi turning about the polar axis with the equator and ecliptic rings, and the inner 四游儀 siyou yi, a double ring carrying the sighting tube and its north-polar-distance scale. This sphere's axis is set to Berkeley's latitude, 37.9° north, and its middle nest turns at the sidereal rate computed from the present moment, so the equator and ecliptic rings stand as the sky stands over Berkeley now; the small disc on the ecliptic ring is the sun at today's longitude. In the 渾天 hun tian model of the heavens the earth sits at the centre like the yolk inside an egg (after Zhang Heng, 渾天儀注 Huntian yi zhu, 2nd century). The longer graduations on the equator ring are the boundaries of the twenty-eight lunar mansions (二十八宿), placed at the right ascensions of their determinative stars (距星) for the epoch J2000; the boundaries have moved with precession since the Song, so the mansion the page names for the sun is approximate.

In 725 the Tang monk Yi Xing (一行) and Liang Lingzan (梁令瓚) built a water-driven sphere with an escapement. In 979, in the Taiping Xingguo era of the Northern Song, Zhang Sixun (張思訓) presented the Taiping Hunyi (太平渾儀), an astronomical clock in the water-driven line, its drive wheel run on mercury instead of water. Su Song (蘇頌, 1020 to 1101), working with Han Gonglian (韓公廉), then built the clock tower 水運儀象臺 Shuiyun yixiang tai in Kaifeng, a wooden pilot model in 1088 and the bronze machinery complete about 1090, whose clockwork turned the bronze armillary sphere on its top platform so that its rings followed the sky; a celestial globe (渾象) sat in the chamber below. He described it in 新儀象法要 Xin yixiang fayao, presented in the 1090s; the illustrations in the printed treatise, transmitted through later editions, are the model for the Woodcut theme. The tower was lost after Kaifeng fell to the Jin in 1127.

Research

An armillary never shows you the sky. It gives a reading through a tube against graduated rings, a structured and lossy record of what is there. That kind of record is what I study.

Across two unrelated domains I keep finding the same problem: the supervision signal is a lossy, biased observation of the thing you actually want to learn. In radiology, free-text reports are a noisy projection of the lesion mask. In ancient languages, a fragmentary lexicon is a noisy projection of the language.

In both cases the field's default assumption, that cleaner labels help, turns out to be wrong or at least unreliable. In both cases the failure is predictable from a measurable property of the supervision. I want to build the general theory and the general fixes.

Thread 01 · Medical imaging

Report-Supervised Tumor Segmentation

UCSF Radiology and Biomedical Imaging

  • Abdominal CT, the CVPR 2027 submission. Learning to segment liver, kidney and pancreas tumors from radiology reports when hand-drawn masks are scarce, and deciding how much each report statement should be trusted; I lead the implementation and experiments.
  • Calibrated report supervision inside the R-Super loss, my honors thesis: separate trust for each kind of report claim (a lesion's size, a hedged finding, a “no tumor” statement), fitted on a small set of masked cases and frozen before training. Each constraint is blended between its strict and relaxed versions by that trust, with unit tests and pre-registered experiments.
  • Prostate MRI. Earlier work: a 12-field LLM extraction schema over 1,471 reports, R-Super ported from CT to MRI, and MedFormer trained to a test Dice of 0.70. The objective reads only lesion size and location, so most extracted fields never reach the model, and unreported lesions raise false positives that best-threshold F1 hides.
Thread 02 · Low-resource NLP

Cross-lingual transfer for cuneiform

Fact Grid Cuneiform, UC Berkeley, with Prof. Adam Anderson

  • Lexicon coverage. First author on the EMNLP 2026 main-conference paper, with its benchmark and the cunei-tools library. Lexicon coverage of the target language predicts Morfessor's transfer, and clamping a high-resource source to a low-resource target's sign inventory shows the effect is causal. The numbers are on the EMNLP card below.
  • OWLID-OCR (in preparation; target ACL 2027). I lead the modeling for an OCR post-correction system for cuneiform: a noisy-channel generative formulation with an open-set unknown-token mechanism and hierarchical pooling across small embedded-language lexica.
  • Pipeline and tagging. An end-to-end pipeline converts noisy transliterations to normalized cuneiform (95%+ conversion on ~2M tokens); a fine-tuned BART seq2seq model handles lemmatization and morphological tagging.

EMNLP 2026 · main conference · oral

What Transfers Across Cuneiform?

C. Zhou and A. Anderson. Proceedings of EMNLP 2026 (main conference, oral presentation).

A benchmark, the cunei-tools library, and the central finding that lexicon coverage of the target language predicts Morfessor's transfer at r = 0.91 across 140 runs (14 source sizes × 5 seeds), while transitional-probability segmentation holds F1 > 0.95 across all six language pairs. The coverage effect is causal: clamping the sign inventory collapses transfer from 0.857 to 0.283, and matched-size controls separate coverage from corpus size.

Noise-Aware Report-Supervised Learning: Decomposing Report-Quality and Extraction Noise

H. Gu, C. Zhou, et al.

Report-supervised tumor segmentation on abdominal CT (liver, kidney, pancreas): learning to segment tumors from radiology reports when hand-drawn masks are scarce, and deciding how much each report statement should be trusted. The pipeline parses 2,760 radiology reports into 7,442 tumor-level records, builds organ masks for 2,764 CT scans, and reproduces R-Super training; the released R-Super loader trains on only about a quarter of report-labeled scans, and ours supervises from all usable reports.
